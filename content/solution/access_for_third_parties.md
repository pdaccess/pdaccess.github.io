## The Third-Party Access Risk

Third-party access is one of the most dangerous blind spots in enterprise security. Contractors, vendors, managed service providers, and business partners need access to your systems to do their jobs — but every third-party credential you share is a potential breach vector.

When a contractor leaves, a vendor relationship ends, or a partner's security posture degrades, your shared credentials become liability. Yet most organizations don't track third-party access, don't limit the scope of privileges, and don't record what these users actually do inside their systems.

PDAccess eliminates this risk by providing granular, time-bound, fully-audited access for every third party — without sharing credentials or compromising security.

## The Third-Party Access Problem

Traditional third-party access management relies on a handful of risky practices:

- **Shared credentials**: A contractor gets the admin password for a system they need to support. Everyone at the vendor company knows it.
- **Permanent access**: Once a contractor gets access, they keep it — even after the project ends or the contract expires.
- **No visibility**: There's no record of what the contractor did, when they logged in, or what commands they executed.
- **Broad privileges**: Contractors are given full admin access when they only need read permissions on specific resources.

These practices don't just create security risks — they violate compliance requirements across PCI-DSS, SOX, HIPAA, and SOC 2.

## How PDAccess Solves Third-Party Access

### Time-Bound Access Policies

When a contractor or vendor needs access, PDAccess creates time-bound policies that automatically expire. A six-month contract gets six months of access — then it's gone, no action required. No credential handoffs. No "just one more extension" requests.

### Granular Role Definition

Not every contractor needs the same access. PDAccess lets you create custom roles that define exactly what each third party can see and do. A billing contractor might access the financial portal but have no visibility into production systems. A DevOps contractor might access specific servers but not databases. The principle of least privilege, enforced automatically.

### Zero-Knowledge Credential Model

PDAccess never shares credentials with third parties. When a contractor connects, they authenticate through their own enterprise credentials (or PDAccess's built-in authentication), and PDAccess handles the connection to the target system using credentials stored in the vault. The contractor never sees, touches, or stores a single password.

### Full Session Recording

Every third-party session is recorded and logged. Administrators can review past sessions, monitor active connections, and revoke access instantly. If something goes wrong during a contractor's session, you have a complete forensic record.

### Multi-Provider Authentication

PDAccess supports multiple authentication providers, so third parties can authenticate using their own enterprise credentials, SSO, or PDAccess-managed accounts. This flexibility means you can onboard partners quickly without compromising security.

## The Business Impact

Organizations using PDAccess for third-party access management achieve:

- **Eliminated credential sharing**: No more passwords sent over email, chat, or spreadsheets
- **Automatic access revocation**: When contracts end, access disappears — no manual process required
- **Complete audit trails**: Every third-party action is recorded and reviewable
- **Compliance-ready**: Meets PCI-DSS requirement 8.1 (unique user IDs) and 10.1 (audit logging) for third-party access
- **Faster onboarding**: New partners can be granted access in minutes, not days

## Bottom Line

Third-party access doesn't have to be a security risk. With PDAccess, you give partners and vendors exactly the access they need — no more, no less — for exactly the time they need it, with complete visibility into what they do. It's the only way to collaborate securely at scale.
